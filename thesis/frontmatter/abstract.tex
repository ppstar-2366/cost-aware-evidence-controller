\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Question answering over scientific papers requires a system to find specific supporting material without passing an entire document to the answer generator for every question. Fixed-depth retrieval allocates the same evidence budget irrespective of whether a question asks for a short definition, a numerical result, a dataset description, or a complex method. This dissertation investigates whether a lightweight and interpretable controller can allocate that budget according to question type while preserving evidence and answer quality.

The project converts each QASPER paper into typed evidence units comprising the abstract, overlapping body-text chunks, table captions, and figure captions. A BM25 ranker supplies lexical candidates. The proposed ControllerV3 begins with three units and executes deterministic expansion rules for definition, data, result, method, complex, and figure-oriented questions. It performs no language-model calls during evidence selection, records every action, and stops after the applicable rule branches complete. The method is therefore a question-aware variable-budget controller, not an iterative estimator of evidence sufficiency.

The frozen controller is evaluated on the complete official QASPER test split of 416 papers and 1,451 questions. On the 1,352 questions with annotated evidence, it obtains an evidence recall of 0.7099 and a question hit rate of 0.8706 at an average estimated evidence cost of 1,638 tokens. The cost-matched BM25 top-7 baseline obtains 0.7059 recall and 0.8624 hit rate at 1,629 estimated tokens. Paper-level paired bootstrap intervals show no clear difference between these methods. Although removing section-aware expansion reduces recall by 0.0220, budget-matched controls show no clear independent aggregate benefit from section targeting; most of the ablation gain is attributable to reading more evidence. A supplementary frozen sample of 201 questions using Qwen2.5-3B similarly yields almost identical answer F1 point estimates for ControllerV3 (0.3280) and BM25 top-7 (0.3278).

The results show that explicit rules can produce heterogeneous and auditable reading budgets under limited compute, but they do not establish an aggregate quality advantage over a well-chosen fixed-depth lexical baseline. The principal contribution is therefore a reproducible analysis of the quality--cost trade-off, together with evidence that cost accounting must distinguish retrieved units, constructed prompts, and actual model tokens.
